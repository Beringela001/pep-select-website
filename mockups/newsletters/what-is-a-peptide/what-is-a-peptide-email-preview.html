Did you know?

A peptide is not a drug.

“Peptide” tells you how a molecule is built. It does not tell you what it is used for. So what is a peptide? A tiny chain made from amino acids.
Fun fact

Nature uses the same 20 common amino-acid building blocks again and again. The order is what changes the message.

Picture a bead bracelet

One bead is an amino acid. A short string of beads is a peptide. Add many more, then fold the chain into a complex shape, and you have a protein.

The three-letter recipe

GHK-Cu

GHK is the recipe: glycine, histidine, and lysine. Those are its three amino acids. Cu is the chemistry symbol for copper.

Researchers study how this tiny chain holds a copper ion and takes part in cell signaling. Its whole name is a molecular clue hiding in plain sight.

Meet the scaffolding

TB-500

Inside cells, actin works like scaffolding: it helps organize shape and movement.

TB-500 is connected with thymosin beta-4 research. Scientists study how these peptide sequences interact with actin. In plain English, they are watching a small chain meet the cell’s support structure.
