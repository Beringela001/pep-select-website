## New Findings (GEO-10+)

**GEO-10 (Low, NEW) — No RSL 1.0 / content-licensing signal.** Searched all 46 raw HTML pages for `rsl.org`, `rel="license"`, and `application/rsl` markup: 0 matches across the entire site. This is distinct from GEO-01 (llms.txt) — RSL (Really Simple Licensing) is a separate, emerging machine-readable standard for declaring AI-training/use permissions at the content level. Its absence means pepselect.com currently makes no explicit machine-readable statement (permissive or restrictive) about AI use of its content, beyond the generic allow-all in robots.txt. Low priority: RSL 1.0 adoption among AI crawlers/LLM providers is still nascent and unconfirmed as an authoritative discovery/ranking input [VERIFY CLAIM — no evidence in this audit's scope of any major AI platform currently consuming RSL signals]; sequence below crawler-token policy (GEO-09) and content-depth work (GEO-03).

**GEO-11 (Low, NEW/methodology note) — `extracted.json`'s `word_count` field overstates true citable content depth.** The crawl tool's `word_count` field (e.g., 979 words for the NAD+ product page, 3250 for the guide) counts all visible DOM text — navigation, footer, legal disclaimer boilerplate, and the full research-gate overlay text — not just the extractable content body. Direct measurement of the actual citable passage on the same NAD+ page (intro + research-context bullets only) is 58 words, roughly 6% of the page's reported `word_count`. This is not a site defect but a measurement-integrity note for future audit cycles: citability scoring (GEO-03 and similar) must be run against a boilerplate-stripped extraction (e.g., trafilatura's `extracted_text`, per the render_page.py tooling spec) rather than raw DOM word counts, or citability will be systematically overstated. No trafilatura-extracted-text field was present in this cycle's `extracted.json`; the direct-measurement approach used for GEO-03 above compensates for this, but the underlying crawl-tool gap should be fixed for future automated GEO-03 scoring to be reliable.

## AI-Crawler Accessibility — Content vs. Gate (confirmed)

All 46 crawled pages show `"gate_present": true` in `extracted.json` — the `#psag-gate` research-gate overlay markup is present site-wide, matching the prior audit's finding that the gate is a rendering/UX concern, not a content-withholding one. Confirmed by direct byte-offset inspection of raw (pre-JS) HTML:

- Homepage (`root.html`, 162,590 bytes total): `<body>` opens at byte 86,180; the page's `<h1>` appears at byte 92,789; the `psag-gate` marker does not appear until byte 143,951 — real page structure and heading content precede the gate markup by roughly 51,000 bytes.
- Product page (`product_nad.html`, 169,833 bytes total): the compound description block (`pepselect-compound__description`) appears at byte 100,691; the `psag-gate` marker appears at byte 151,145 — the actual product/compound content precedes the gate by roughly 50,000 bytes.

This confirms an AI crawler that reads raw, non-JS-rendered HTML (which GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, and OAI-SearchBot all do for indexing/retrieval) has full access to real page substance — headings, product descriptions, batch data — regardless of the gate's presence later in the same document. `Content-Type: text/html` and no SPA-shell indicators were observed in the response headers sampled (`root.hdr`, guide `.hdr`), consistent with server-side rendering rather than a client-side-only shell. This is a content-accessibility confirmation, not a UX endorsement — the gate's visual/interaction impact on human and JS-rendering-dependent visitors is out of this specialist's scope (see VIS/SXO findings).
